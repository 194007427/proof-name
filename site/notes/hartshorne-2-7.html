Determining rational maps from divisors

Weil divisor

Let XX be a normal kk-variety, DD be a Weil divisor on XX. Then we have the following vector space of rational functions Γ(X,D):={f∈K(X)*|D+div⁡(f)≥0}∪{0}.\Gamma(X,D):=\{f\in K(X)^* | D+\operatorname{div}(f)\ge 0 \}\cup \{0\}. Then each s∈Γ(X,D)s\in \Gamma(X,D) corresponds to an effective divisor DsD_s linearly equivalent to DD; that is Ds:=D+div⁡(s).D_s:=D+\operatorname{div}(s). Let V⊂Γ(X,D)V\subset \Gamma(X,D) be a vector subspace, we can define the base locus of VV.

Definition 1.1. With X,DX,D and VV as above, we define the base locus of VV as follows Bs⁡(V):=⋂s∈VSupp⁡Ds.\operatorname{Bs}(V):=\bigcap_{s\in V}\operatorname{Supp}D_s. In particularly, we denote Bs⁡(Γ(X,D))\operatorname{Bs}(\Gamma(X,D)) by Bs⁡(D)\operatorname{Bs}(D). We say DD is base-point-free (bpf, for short) if Bs⁡(D)=∅\operatorname{Bs}(D)=\emptyset.

One fact needs to be clarified.

Lemma 1.2. Let X,DX,D and VV be as above, and let {si}⊂V\{s_i\}\subset V be the generators. Then we have the following equality ⋂s∈VSupp⁡Ds=⋂iSupp⁡Dsi.\bigcap_{s\in V}\operatorname{Supp}D_s=\bigcap_{i}\operatorname{Supp}D_{s_i}. Note that if s=0s=0, then Supp⁡(Ds)=X\operatorname{Supp}(D_{s})=X.

Proof. It is sufficient to prove the inclusion ⊃\supset. Let x∈⋂iSupp⁡Dsix\in \bigcap_{i}\operatorname{Supp}D_{s_i}. If there exists a rational function ss such that x∉Supp⁡(Ds)x\notin \operatorname{Supp}(D_s), then there exists an open neighborhood UU of xx such that D|U+div⁡(s)|U=0D|_U+\operatorname{div}(s)|_U=0 and hence D|U=−div⁡(s)|UD|_U=-\operatorname{div}(s)|_U. Note that: x∈⋂iSupp⁡Dsi⇔x∈⋂iSupp⁡(−div⁡(s)|U+div⁡(si)|U)⇔sis(x)=0 for all i.\begin{align*} x\in \bigcap_{i}\operatorname{Supp}D_{s_i} &\iff x\in \bigcap_{i}\operatorname{Supp}(-\operatorname{div}(s)|_U+\operatorname{div}(s_i)|_U) \\ &\iff \frac{s_i}{s}(x)=0 \text{ for all } i. \end{align*} Setting s=∑aisis=\sum a_is_i, then we have 1=ss(x)=∑aisis(x)=0.\begin{align*} 1=\frac{s}{s}(x)=\frac{\sum a_is_i}{s}(x)=0. \end{align*} A contradiction. ◻

Corollary 1.4. Let XX be a normal kk-variety, DD be a Weil divisor on XX. Then DD bpf implies that DD is a Cariter divisor.

This is the reason why we do not talk about base-point-free Weil divisors.

Remark 1.5. X\Bs⁡(V)X\setminus \operatorname{Bs}(V) is not the largest locus where ϕV\phi_V can be defined. For example, let EE be an elliptic curve and P∈EP\in E be a closed point. Spectrum and projective spectrum of quasi-coherent algebras

The symmetric algebra of an 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-module

Let’s recall the tensor algebra and the symmetric algebra of a module. Let AA be a ring and let MM be an AA-module. The tensor algebra of MM is the (in general non-commutative) AA-algebra T(M):=TA(M):=⨁n≥0Tn(M),with Tn(M):=M⊗n:=M⊗A⋯⊗AM,T(M):=T_A(M):=\bigoplus_{n\ge 0}T^n(M), \quad \text{with } T^n(M):=M^{\otimes n}:=M\otimes_A \cdots \otimes_A M, where the product is given by (m1⊗⋯⊗mn,m1′⊗⋯⊗n′′)↦m1⊗⋯⊗mn⊗m1′⊗⋯⊗n′′.(m_1\otimes\cdots\otimes m_n,m'_1\otimes\cdots\otimes'_{n'})\mapsto m_1\otimes\cdots\otimes m_n\otimes m'_1\otimes\cdots\otimes'_{n'}. This is a graded AA-algebra with T0(M)=AT_0(M)=A and T1(M)=MT^1(M)=M.

The symmetric algebra of MM is the quotient T(M)/IT(M)/I, where II is the ideal of T(M)T(M) generated by the elements m⊗m′−m′⊗mm\otimes m'-m'\otimes m for m,m′∈Mm,m'\in M. It is denoted by Sym⁡A(M)\operatorname{Sym}_A(M) or Sym⁡(M)\operatorname{Sym}(M). This is a graded commutative AA-algebra, Sym⁡(M)=⨁n≥0Sym⁡n(M)\operatorname{Sym}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\ge 0}\operatorname{Sym}^n(M). We have Sym⁡0(M)=A\operatorname{Sym}^0(M)=A and Sym⁡1(M)=M\operatorname{Sym}^1(M)=M.

Remark 2.1. The symmetric algebra Sym⁡(M)\operatorname{Sym}(M) has the following properties

(1) We have an AA-linear map ι:Sym⁡1(M)↪Sym⁡(M)\iota:\operatorname{Sym}^1(M)\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(M). For each AA-algebra BB, the map ι\iota induces an isomorphism Hom⁡(A-𝖠𝗅𝗀)(Sym⁡(M),B)≅Hom⁡(A-𝖬𝗈𝖽)(M,B),φ↦φ∘ι.\operatorname{Hom}_{(A\text{-}\mathsf{Alg})}(\operatorname{Sym}(M),B)\cong \operatorname{Hom}_{(A\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})}(M,B), \quad \varphi\mapsto \varphi\circ\iota.

(2) If φ:A→B\varphi:A\to B is a ring homomorphism, then we have an isomorphism of BB-algebras Sym⁡A(M)⊗AB≅Sym⁡B(M⊗AB).\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)\otimes_A B\cong \operatorname{Sym}_B(M\otimes_A B).

(3) Let MM and M′M' be AA-modules, then we have Sym⁡(M⊕M′)≅Sym⁡(M)⊗ASym⁡(M′).\operatorname{Sym}(M\oplus M')\cong \operatorname{Sym}(M)\otimes_A \operatorname{Sym}(M'). In particularly, if MM is a free AA-module with basis (x1,…,xr)(x_1,\dots,x_r), then we obtain an isomorphism of graded AA-algebras A[T1,…,Tr]≅Sym⁡(M),with Ti↦xi.A[T_1,\dots, T_r]\cong \operatorname{Sym}(M), \quad \text{with } T_i\mapsto x_i. Sym⁡n(M)\operatorname{Sym}^n(M) is a free AA-module of rank (r+n−1n)\binom{r+n-1}{n}.

Let XX be a scheme and ℰ\mathcal{E} be a quasi-coherent 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-module. For each affine open subset U⊂XU\subset X, we have a commutative graded 𝒪U\mathcal{O}_U-algebra associated to the algebra Sym⁡Γ(U,X)(Γ(ℰ,U))\operatorname{Sym}_{\Gamma(U,X)}(\Gamma(\mathcal{E},U)). These algebras glue together to form a quasi-coherent 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-algebra, which we denote by Sym⁡(ℰ)\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}). We call it the symmetric algebra of ℰ\mathcal{E}. Note that Sym⁡0(ℰ)=𝒪X\operatorname{Sym}^0(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{O}_X and Sym⁡1(ℰ)=ℰ\operatorname{Sym}^1(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{E}.

Remark 2.2. The symmetric algebra Sym⁡(ℰ)\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}) has the following properties

(1) We have an 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-linear map ι:Sym⁡1(ℰ)↪Sym⁡(ℰ)\iota:\operatorname{Sym}^1(\mathcal{E})\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}). For each 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-algebra 𝒜\mathcal{A}, the map ι\iota induces an isomorphism Hom⁡(𝒪X-𝖠𝗅𝗀)(Sym⁡(ℰ),𝒜)≅Hom⁡(𝒪X-𝖬𝗈𝖽)(ℰ,𝒜),φ↦φ∘ι.\operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathcal{O}_X\text{-}\mathsf{Alg})}(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}),\mathcal{A})\cong \operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathcal{O}_X\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{A}), \quad \varphi\mapsto \varphi\circ\iota.

(2) If f:T→Xf:T\to X is a morphism of schemes, then we have an isomorphism of 𝒪T\mathcal{O}_T-algebras f*Sym⁡(ℰ)≅Sym⁡(f*ℰ).f^*\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E})\cong \operatorname{Sym}(f^*\mathcal{E}).

(3) Let ℰ\mathcal{E} and ℱ\mathcal{F} be 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-modules, then we have Sym⁡(ℰ⊕ℱ)≅Sym⁡(ℰ)⊗𝒪XSym⁡(ℱ).\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}\oplus \mathcal{F})\cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{F}). In particularly, if ℰ≅𝒪X⊕r\mathcal{E}\cong \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus r}, then we obtain an isomorphism of 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-algebras 𝒪X[T1,…,Tr]≅Sym⁡(ℰ).\mathcal{O}_X[T_1,\dots, T_r]\cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{E}). Sym⁡n(ℰ)\operatorname{Sym}^n(\mathcal{E}) is a free 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-module of rank (r+n−1n)\binom{r+n-1}{n}.

Remark 2.3. Let XX be a scheme and ℒ\mathcal{L} be a line bundle. Then we have an isomorphism of graded 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-algebras. T(ℒ):=⨁n≥0ℒ⊗n≅Sym⁡(ℒ).T(\mathcal{L}):=\bigoplus_{n\ge 0}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}\cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{L}). In fact, let ee be the local generator of ℒ\mathcal{L}. For each x,y∈ℒx,y\in \mathcal{L}, they can be written as x=f⋅e,y=g⋅e.x=f\cdot e, \quad y=g\cdot e. Hence, we have x⊗y=(fg)e⊗e=(gf)e⊗e=y⊗x.x\otimes y=(fg)e\otimes e=(gf)e\otimes e=y\otimes x.

Therefore, they glue together to form an invertible sheaf 𝒪Proj⁡𝒜(1)\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}}(1) on Proj⁡𝒜\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}.

Proposition 2.5. Let ℒ\mathcal{L} be a line bundle, 𝒜\mathcal{A} be a graded quasi-coherent 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-algebra. We can define a new graded quasi-coherent 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X-algebra 𝒜′\mathcal{A}' as follows 𝒜n′=𝒜n⊗ℒ⊗n,(a⊗t1⊗⋯⊗tn,a′⊗t1′⊗⋯⊗tn′′)↦(a⋅a′)⊗t1⊗⋯⊗tn⊗t1′⊗⋯⊗tn′′.\begin{align*} \mathcal{A}'_n&=\mathcal{A}_n\otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n},\\ (a\otimes t_1\otimes\cdots\otimes t_n,a'\otimes t'_1\otimes\cdots\otimes t'_{n'})&\mapsto (a\cdot a')\otimes t_1\otimes\cdots\otimes t_n\otimes t'_1\otimes\cdots\otimes t'_{n'}. \end{align*} It is a commutative graded algebra by Remark 13. Set P=Proj⁡𝒜P=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}, Pℒ=Proj⁡𝒜′P_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}' and let π:P′→X\pi:P'\to X be the structure morphism. There is an isomorphism of XX-schemes φ:Pℒ→∼P\varphi:P_{\mathcal{L}}\xrightarrow{\sim} P such that 𝒪Proj⁡𝒜′(1)=φ*𝒪Proj⁡𝒜(1)⊗π*ℒ\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}'}(1)= \varphi^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\pi^*\mathcal{L}.

Proof. Let {Ui}\{U_i\} be an open covering of XX such that there exist isomorphism ti:ℒ|Ui→∼𝒪Uit_i:\mathcal{L}|_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}_{U_i} for all ii. Set 𝒜i:=𝒜|Ui\mathcal{A}_i:=\mathcal{A}|_{U_i} and 𝒜i′:=𝒜′|Ui\mathcal{A}'_i:=\mathcal{A}'|_{U_i}. We have isomorphisms gi:𝒜i→∼𝒜i′g_i:\mathcal{A}_i\xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{A}_i'. Any other choice of isomorphisms ℒ|Ui→∼𝒪Ui\mathcal{L}|_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}_{U_i} differs from tit_i by an invertible section in Γ(Ui,𝒪X×)\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal{O}_X^{\times}). Therefore the corresponding isomorphism 𝒜i→𝒜i′\mathcal{A}_i\to \mathcal{A}'_i differs from gig_i by multiplication with fidf_i^d in degree dd. This does not change the induced isomorphism φi:Proj⁡𝒜i→∼Proj⁡𝒜i′\varphi_i:\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}_i\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}'_i and hence these local isomorphisms glue together to a global isomorphism φ:Proj⁡𝒜′→Proj⁡𝒜\varphi:\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}'\to \mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}. Suppose the transition functions of ℒ\mathcal{L} are given by fi/fj∈Γ(Ui∩Uj,𝒪X×)f_i/f_j\in\Gamma(U_i\cap U_j,\mathcal{O}_X^{\times}). Since 𝒪Proj⁡𝒜′(1)\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}'}(1) differs from φ*𝒪Proj⁡𝒜(1)\varphi^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}}(1) by an invertible sheaf whose transition functions are given by π*(fi)/π*(fj)\pi^*(f_i)/\pi^*(f_j), we obtain that 𝒪Proj⁡𝒜′(1)=φ*𝒪Proj⁡𝒜(1)⊗π*ℒ.\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}'}(1)=\varphi^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes \pi^*\mathcal{L}. ◻

Lemma 3.1. Let f:X→Sf:X\to S be a projective morphism between varieties and let ℒ\mathcal{L} be a line bundle that is ff-ample. Then there is an isomorphism X≅Proj⁡⨁d≥0f*ℒ⊗n.X\cong \mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{d\ge 0}f_*\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}.

Proof. (Görtz and Wedhorn 2020, Proposition 13.74). ◻

Lemma 3.2. Let RR be a ring and let AA be a finitely generated graded RR-algebra with A0=RA_0=R. Then there exists a finitely generated graded RR-algebra A′A' with A0′=RA'_0=R which is generated by finitely many elements of A1′A'_1 such that the RR-schemes Proj⁡A\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}A and Proj⁡A′\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}A' are isomorphic.

Proof. (Görtz and Wedhorn 2020, Remark 13.11). ◻

Lemma 3.3. Let XX be a quasi-projective kk-variety with ample divisor HH and let ℱ\mathcal{F} be a coherent subsheaf of 𝒦\mathcal{K}. Then ℱ(−nH)\mathcal{F}(-nH) is a sheaf of ideal in 𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X for nn sufficiently large.

Proof. Let 𝒥\mathcal{J} be the ideal sheaf of denominators of ℱ\mathcal{F}, defined locally as {a∈𝒪X|a⋅ℱ∈𝒪X}.\{a\in\mathcal{O}_X|a\cdot \mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{O}_X\}. This is a nonzero coherent sheaf of ideals on XX. Since HH is ample, we have an injection 𝒪X→𝒥(nH),for n sufficiently large.\mathcal{O}_X\to \mathcal{J}(nH), \quad \text{for } n \text{ sufficiently large}. And hence an injection 𝒪X(−nH)→𝒥.\mathcal{O}_X(-nH)\to \mathcal{J}. Viewing 𝒪X(−nH)\mathcal{O}_X(-nH) as a subsheaf of ℐ\mathcal{I}, we see that 𝒪X(−nH)⋅ℱ=ℱ(−nH)⊂𝒪X\mathcal{O}_X(-nH)\cdot\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(-nH)\subset \mathcal{O}_X. ◻

Theorem 3.4. Let XX be a quasi-projective variety over kk. If ZZ is another variety and f:Z→Xf:Z\to X is any birational projective morphism, then there exists a coherent sheaf of ideals 𝒥\mathcal{J} on XX such that ZZ is isomorphic to the blowing-up X̃\tilde{X} of XX with respect to 𝒥\mathcal{J}, and ff corresponds to π:X̃→X\pi:\tilde{X}\to X under this isomorphism.

Proof. Let ℒ⊂𝒦Z\mathcal{L}\subset \mathcal{K}_Z be an ff-ample line bundle on ZZ, then Z≅Proj⁡⨁d≥0f*ℒnZ\cong \mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{d\ge 0}f_*\mathcal{L}^{n} by Lemma 16. Since ff is birational, we have f*ℒ⊂𝒦Xf_*\mathcal{L}\subset \mathcal{K}_X. Let 𝒜\mathcal{A} denote the graded algebra ⨁d≥0f*ℒn\bigoplus_{d\ge 0}f_*\mathcal{L}^{n}, don’t forget that 𝒜\mathcal{A} is generated by 𝒜1\mathcal{A}_1. Example 3.5. Let XX be a kk-variety, D={(Ui,fi)}D=\{(U_i,f_i)\} be a Cartier divisor. Let s0,⋯,sn∈Γ(X,D)s_0,\cdots,s_n\in\Gamma(X,D) be rational functions with base locus BB and let U=X\BU=X\setminus B. Then we have a map φ:U→ℙn\varphi:U\to \mathbb{P}^n induced by sis_i.
