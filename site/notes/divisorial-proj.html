Divisorial Proj

PDF · LaTeX source

Let XX be a normal integral variety over a field kk, and let BB be an integral Weil divisor on XX. Assume that the divisorial algebra ℛ(X,B):=⨁m≥0𝒪X(mB)\mathcal R(X,B):=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_X(mB) is finitely generated as an 𝒪X\mathcal O_X-algebra, and set f:Z=Proj⁡Xℛ(X,B)→X.f\colon Z=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R(X,B)\longrightarrow X. This note studies the properties of ff and proves the following:

  1. The morphism ff detects the non-ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of BB: its isomorphism locus on XX is precisely the ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of BB. In particular, f−1(U)→Uf^{-1}(U)\to U is an isomorphism, where U⊆XU\subseteq X is the smooth locus.

  2. The variety ZZ is normal.

  3. The morphism ff is small. Consequently, the strict transform BZB_Z of BB is ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier and ff-ample.

0The twisting sheaf on Proj

Let S=⨁n≥0SnS=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_n be a nonnegatively graded ring, and set P=Proj⁡SP=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}S. For a graded SS-module MM, let M̃\widetilde M denote the associated quasicoherent sheaf on PP, characterized on standard affine opens by Γ(D+(s),M̃)=(Ms)0,\Gamma(D_+(s),\widetilde M)=(M_s)_0, where ss is homogeneous of positive degree. Define the shifted graded module S(1)S(1) by S(1)n=Sn+1.S(1)_n=S_{n+1}. The twisting sheaf on PP is defined as 𝒪P(1):=S(1)̃.\mathcal O_P(1):=\widetilde{S(1)}. In particular, Γ(D+(s),𝒪P(1))=(S(1)s)0=(Ss)1.\Gamma(D_+(s),\mathcal O_P(1)) =(S(1)_s)_0=(S_s)_1.

Lemma 1. If SS is generated by S1S_1 as an S0S_0-algebra, then 𝒪P(1)\mathcal O_P(1) is an invertible sheaf.

Proof. We first show that the standard opens D+(s)D_+(s), with s∈S1s\in S_1, cover PP. Indeed, if a homogeneous prime ideal 𝔭\mathfrak p contains S1S_1, then generation in degree one implies that it contains S+S_+. Such a prime does not belong to Proj⁡S\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}S. Hence every point of PP lies in D+(s)D_+(s) for some s∈S1s\in S_1.

Fix such an ss. Since ss is invertible in SsS_s and has degree one, multiplication by ss gives an isomorphism of (Ss)0(S_s)_0-modules (Ss)0→∼(Ss)1,a↦sa,(S_s)_0\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }(S_s)_1, \qquad a\longmapsto sa, whose inverse is multiplication by s−1s^{-1}. Sheafifying on D+(s)=Spec⁡(Ss)0D_+(s)=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(S_s)_0 therefore yields 𝒪D+(s)≃𝒪P(1)|D+(s).\mathcal O_{D_+(s)} \simeq\mathcal O_P(1)|_{D_+(s)}. Since these opens cover PP, the sheaf 𝒪P(1)\mathcal O_P(1) is locally free of rank one. ◻

The sheaf 𝒪P(1)\mathcal O_P(1) need not be invertible for an arbitrary grading, as the following example shows.

Example 2. Let S=k[u]S=k[u] with deg⁡u=2\deg u=2. Localizing at uu preserves the original grading: deg⁡(uj)=2j\deg(u^j)=2j for every j∈ℤj\in\mathbb Z. Thus Su=k[u,u−1]=⋯⊕ku−1⊕k⊕ku⊕ku2⊕⋯,S_u=k[u,u^{-1}] =\cdots\oplus ku^{-1}\oplus k\oplus ku\oplus ku^2\oplus\cdots, where the displayed summands have degrees …,−2,0,2,4,…\ldots,-2,0,2,4,\ldots, respectively. In particular, only even degrees occur, and (Su)0=k,(Su)1=0,(Su)2=ku≃k.(S_u)_0=k,\qquad (S_u)_1=0,\qquad (S_u)_2=ku\simeq k. Consequently, P=D+(u)=Spec⁡(Su)0≃Spec⁡k,Γ(P,𝒪P(1))=(Su)1=0.P=D_+(u)=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(S_u)_0\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k, \qquad \Gamma(P,\mathcal O_P(1))=(S_u)_1=0. Thus 𝒪P(1)\mathcal O_P(1) is the zero sheaf on the nonempty scheme PP, and is not invertible.

1The isomorphism locus

Proposition 3. Let XX be a normal integral variety and BB an integral Weil divisor on XX. Assume that ℛ=ℛ(X,B)=⨁m≥0𝒪X(mB)\mathcal R=\mathcal R(X,B)=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_X(mB) is finitely generated, and let f:Z=Proj⁡Xℛ→X.f\colon Z=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R\longrightarrow X. Choose r>0r>0 such that the Veronese algebra ℛ(r)=⨁n≥0𝒪X(nrB)\mathcal R^{(r)}=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal O_X(nrB) is generated in degree one. Identifying ZZ with Proj⁡Xℛ(r)\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R^{(r)}, Lemma 1 shows that L:=𝒪Proj⁡Xℛ(r)(1)L:=\mathcal O_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R^{(r)}}(1) is a line bundle on ZZ. For every point x∈Xx\in X, the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. BB is ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier at xx;

  2. there is an open neighborhood VV of xx such that f−1(V)→Vf^{-1}(V)\to V is an isomorphism;

  3. the fiber f−1(x)f^{-1}(x) has dimension zero.

Proof. (1)⇒\Rightarrow(2). Choose a neighborhood VV of xx and q>0q>0 such that qB|VqB|_V is Cartier. Then f−1(V)≃Proj⁡V⨁m≥0𝒪V(mqB)≃Proj⁡VSym⁡𝒪V𝒪V(qB)≃V.f^{-1}(V) \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_V\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_V(mqB) \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_V\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_V}\mathcal O_V(qB) \simeq V.

(2)⇒\Rightarrow(1). Identify f−1(V)f^{-1}(V) with VV. The line bundle L|f−1(V)L|_{f^{-1}(V)} agrees with 𝒪V(rB)\mathcal O_V(rB) on the regular locus of VV. Both sheaves are reflexive and VV is normal, so this isomorphism extends to VV. Thus 𝒪V(rB)\mathcal O_V(rB) is invertible and BB is ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier at xx.

The implication (2)⇒\Rightarrow(3) is immediate. For (3)⇒\Rightarrow(2), since ff is projective and birational, upper semicontinuity of fiber dimension gives a neighborhood VV of xx over which ff is quasi-finite [SP, Tag 0D4I]. There ff is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite [SP, Tag 02LS], and therefore an isomorphism because VV is normal [SP, Tag 0AB1]. ◻

In particular, if Iso⁡X(f):={x∈X∣f is an isomorphism over a neighborhood of x},\operatorname{Iso}_X(f) :=\{x\in X\mid f\text{ is an isomorphism over a neighborhood of }x\}, then Proposition 3 identifies this set with the ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of BB. Its complement is X\Iso⁡X(f)={x∈X∣B is not ℚ-Cartier at x}={x∈X∣dim⁡f−1(x)≥1}.\begin{aligned} X\setminus\operatorname{Iso}_X(f) &=\{x\in X\mid B\text{ is not }\mathbb Q\text{-Cartier at }x\}\\ &=\{x\in X\mid\dim f^{-1}(x)\ge1\}. \end{aligned} Since Iso⁡X(f)\operatorname{Iso}_X(f) is open by definition, Proposition 3 also shows that the non-ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of BB is closed.

We give a direct proof of this closedness, independent of Proposition 3 and of finite generation. Write UB={x∈X∣B is Cartier at x}.U_B=\{x\in X\mid B\text{ is Cartier at }x\}.

Lemma 4. The Cartier locus UBU_B is open in XX. Equivalently, the non-Cartier locus X\UBX\setminus U_B is closed.

Proof. Fix x∈UBx\in U_B. A Cartier divisor on the spectrum of a local ring is principal, since every invertible module over a local ring is free of rank one. Hence there is a rational function g∈K(X)*g\in K(X)^* such that B|Spec⁡𝒪X,x=div⁡Spec⁡𝒪X,x(g).B|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_{X,x}} =\operatorname{div}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_{X,x}}(g). Set E=B−div⁡X(g)E=B-\operatorname{div}_X(g). The prime divisors of XX that contain xx correspond to the height-one primes of 𝒪X,x\mathcal O_{X,x}, with the same discrete valuations. The displayed equality therefore says that the coefficient of EE along every prime divisor containing xx is zero. Thus x∉Supp⁡Ex\notin\mathop{\mathrm{Supp}}E.

The support of a Weil divisor is closed, so V=X\Supp⁡EV=X\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Supp}}E is an open neighborhood of xx. On VV we have B|V=div⁡V(g),B|_V=\operatorname{div}_V(g), and consequently V⊆UBV\subseteq U_B. This proves that UBU_B is open. ◻

Corollary 5. The ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus UBℚ={x∈X∣mB is Cartier at x for some integer m≥1}U_B^{\mathbb Q} =\{x\in X\mid mB\text{ is Cartier at }x \text{ for some integer }m\ge1\} is open. In particular, the non-ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of BB is closed.

Proof. We have UBℚ=⋃m≥1UmBU_B^{\mathbb Q}=\bigcup_{m\ge1}U_{mB}, and each UmBU_{mB} is open by Lemma 4. ◻

Remark 6 (Locally factorial and ℚ\mathbb Q-factorial loci). The analogous openness questions for local factoriality and ℚ\mathbb Q-factoriality concern all Weil divisors simultaneously. Their loci are the intersections of the Cartier loci, respectively the ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier loci, of all Weil divisors. Since these intersections can be infinite, their openness does not follow formally from Lemma 4 and Corollary 5.

For a normal variety over an algebraically closed field, both loci are open, by Boissière–Gabber–Serman [BGS, Theorems 4.2 and 6.1]; hence their complements are closed. Here a variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over the ground field, and these results hold in arbitrary characteristic. The non-ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of a fixed divisor BB is contained in the non-ℚ\mathbb Q-factorial locus, but can be strictly smaller: for instance, B=0B=0 is Cartier everywhere.

2Normality of the divisorial Proj

We use the isomorphism over the regular locus to prove that ZZ is normal.

Proposition 7. Let XX be a normal integral variety and BB an integral Weil divisor such that ℛ(X,B)=⨁m≥0𝒪X(mB)\mathcal R(X,B)=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_X(mB) is finitely generated. Set f:Z=Proj⁡Xℛ(X,B)→X.f\colon Z=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R(X,B)\longrightarrow X. Choose r>0r>0 such that ℛ(X,B)(r)\mathcal R(X,B)^{(r)} is generated in degree one, and let L:=𝒪Proj⁡Xℛ(X,B)(r)(1)L:=\mathcal O_{\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R(X,B)^{(r)}}(1) be the tautological line bundle on Z≃Proj⁡Xℛ(X,B)(r)Z\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R(X,B)^{(r)}. Then ZZ is normal, and the canonical maps give isomorphisms 𝒪X(mrB)→∼f*L⊗m(m≥0).\mathcal O_X(mrB)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }f_*L^{\otimes m} \qquad(m\ge0).

Proof. Let ν:W→Z\nu\colon W\to Z be the normalization, and set g=f∘ν:W→X,L′=ν*L.g=f\circ\nu\colon W\to X, \qquad L'=\nu^*L. Since ZZ is a variety, ν\nu is finite [SP, Tag 0BXR]. Hence gg is proper, and L′L' is gg-ample: over each affine open subset of XX, it is the pullback of an ample line bundle by a finite morphism.

Let U=XregU=X_{\mathrm{reg}} and j:U↪Xj\colon U\hookrightarrow X be the inclusion. Over an algebraically closed field, UU is the smooth locus. Since XX is normal, X\UX\setminus U has codimension at least two. Every Weil divisor on UU is Cartier, so Proposition 3 identifies f−1(U)f^{-1}(U) with UU. The normalization is also an isomorphism there. Under these identifications, L|f−1(U)≃𝒪U(rB),L′|g−1(U)≃𝒪U(rB).L|_{f^{-1}(U)}\simeq\mathcal O_U(rB), \qquad L'|_{g^{-1}(U)}\simeq\mathcal O_U(rB).

Fix m≥0m\ge0. Restriction to the dense open subset g−1(U)g^{-1}(U) gives an injection g*(L′)⊗m↪j*𝒪U(mrB)=𝒪X(mrB).g_*(L')^{\otimes m} \hookrightarrow j_*\mathcal O_U(mrB) =\mathcal O_X(mrB). The injection holds because WW is integral and (L′)⊗m(L')^{\otimes m} is invertible; the last equality follows from reflexivity of 𝒪X(mrB)\mathcal O_X(mrB) and the codimension of X\UX\setminus U. In the other direction, the defining graded algebra and pullback by ν\nu give natural maps 𝒪X(mrB)→f*L⊗m→g*(L′)⊗m.\mathcal O_X(mrB)\longrightarrow f_*L^{\otimes m} \longrightarrow g_*(L')^{\otimes m}. The second map is injective because ν\nu is birational and ZZ is integral. The composite of these maps with the preceding injection into 𝒪X(mrB)\mathcal O_X(mrB) is the identity: each section restricts to the same rational function on UU. It follows that all the maps are isomorphisms. In particular, ⨁m≥0g*(L′)⊗m≃⨁m≥0𝒪X(mrB)=ℛ(r) \bigoplus_{m\ge0}g_*(L')^{\otimes m} \simeq\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_X(mrB) =\mathcal R^{(r)} \tag{1} as graded 𝒪X\mathcal O_X-algebras; the identifications respect multiplication because they are induced by restriction to UU.

A proper morphism equipped with a relatively ample line bundle is recovered as the relative Proj of its section algebra [SP, Tag 0C6J]. Applying this to (g,L′)(g,L') and using (1), we obtain W→∼Proj⁡X⨁m≥0g*(L′)⊗m≃Proj⁡Xℛ(r)=Z.W\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\bigoplus_{m\ge0}g_*(L')^{\otimes m} \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_X\mathcal R^{(r)}=Z. The resulting morphism W→ZW\to Z is ν\nu, since it is induced by the pullbacks of the defining homogeneous sections. Thus ν\nu is an isomorphism and ZZ is normal. The asserted direct-image identities were established by the same comparison. ◻

3Smallness

To prepare for the proof of smallness, we express ff locally as a blowup.

On an affine open subset V⊆XV\subseteq X, choose an effective Cartier divisor HH such that J:=𝒪V(rB−H)⊆𝒪VJ:=\mathcal O_V(rB-H)\subseteq\mathcal O_V is an ideal sheaf. Since ℛ(r)\mathcal R^{(r)} is generated in degree one, 𝒪V(rB)m=𝒪V(mrB)(m≥0).\mathcal O_V(rB)^m=\mathcal O_V(mrB)\qquad(m\ge0). Hence Jm=𝒪V(rB)m⊗𝒪V(−mH)=𝒪V(mrB−mH),J^m=\mathcal O_V(rB)^m\otimes\mathcal O_V(-mH) =\mathcal O_V(mrB-mH), and ⨁m≥0Jm=⨁m≥0𝒪V(mrB)⊗𝒪V(−H)⊗m.\bigoplus_{m\ge0}J^m =\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_V(mrB)\otimes\mathcal O_V(-H)^{\otimes m}. Twisting by a line bundle does not change relative Proj [SP, Tag 02NC], so Bl⁡JV=Proj⁡V⨁m≥0Jm≃Proj⁡V(ℛ(r)|V)≃f−1(V). \operatorname{Bl}_J V =\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_V\bigoplus_{m\ge0}J^m \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}_V(\mathcal R^{(r)}|_V) \simeq f^{-1}(V). \tag{2} By Proposition 3, its non-isomorphism locus is the non-ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of B|VB|_V.

Proposition 8. The morphism ff is small. If BZB_Z denotes the strict transform of BB, then L≃𝒪Z(rBZ).L\simeq\mathcal O_Z(rB_Z). In particular, BZB_Z is ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier and ff-ample.

Proof. Smallness is local on XX, so work over an affine open subset and use the blowup presentation above. Set D=H−rB≥0,J=𝒪X(−D),D=H-rB\ge0,\qquad J=\mathcal O_X(-D), and define the effective Cartier divisor FF by J𝒪Z=𝒪Z(−F).J\mathcal O_Z=\mathcal O_Z(-F). Then Jm=𝒪X(−mD)J^m=\mathcal O_X(-mD) for all m≥0m\ge0, and 𝒪Z(−F)\mathcal O_Z(-F) is ff-ample. On the big open set U=XregU=X_{\mathrm{reg}}, the morphism ff is an isomorphism and FF agrees with DD. Thus (f*F)|U=D|U(f_*F)|_U=D|_U, and hence f*F=D.f_*F=D.

Suppose that ff contracts a prime divisor EE. Since ZZ is normal and 𝒪Z(−F)\mathcal O_Z(-F) is ample over the affine base, 𝒪Z(−mF+E)\mathcal O_Z(-mF+E) is globally generated for m≫0m\gg0 [SP, Tag 01Q3]. Choose a section that does not vanish at ηE\eta_E and represent it by φ∈K(X)*\varphi\in K(X)^*. Then C:=div⁡Z(φ)−mF+E≥0,coeff⁡EC=0.C:=\operatorname{div}_Z(\varphi)-mF+E\ge0, \qquad \operatorname{coeff}_E C=0. Consequently, coeff⁡E(div⁡Z(φ)−mF)=−1. \operatorname{coeff}_E\bigl(\operatorname{div}_Z(\varphi)-mF\bigr)=-1. \tag{3} Pushing CC down to XX and using f*E=0f_*E=0 gives div⁡X(φ)−mD=f*C≥0.\operatorname{div}_X(\varphi)-mD=f_*C\ge0. Therefore φ∈H0(X,𝒪X(−mD))=H0(X,Jm).\varphi\in H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(-mD))=H^0(X,J^m). Since Jm𝒪Z=𝒪Z(−mF)J^m\mathcal O_Z=\mathcal O_Z(-mF), it follows that div⁡Z(φ)−mF≥0,\operatorname{div}_Z(\varphi)-mF\ge0, contradicting (3). Thus ff is small.

It follows that F=DZF=D_Z and f*H=HZf^*H=H_Z, where subscripts denote strict transforms. The formula for the tautological line bundle under twisting now gives L≃𝒪Z(f*H−F)=𝒪Z(HZ−DZ)=𝒪Z(rBZ).L\simeq\mathcal O_Z(f^*H-F) =\mathcal O_Z(H_Z-D_Z) =\mathcal O_Z(rB_Z). These local identifications are induced by the rational realizations of the sheaves and hence glue. Since LL is ff-ample, BZB_Z is ℚ\mathbb Q-Cartier and ff-ample. ◻
