Negativity Lemma

Theorem

Let f:X→Yf:X\to Y be a proper birational morphism between normal varieties. Let DD be a ℝ\mathbb R-Cartier divisor on XX such that −D-D is nef over YY. Then:

  1. DD is effective if and only if f*Df_*D is effective.

  2. Assume that DD is effective. Then for every y∈Yy\in Y, either f−1(y)∩Supp⁡(D)=∅f^{-1}(y)\cap \operatorname{Supp}(D)=\emptyset or f−1(y)⊂Supp⁡(D)f^{-1}(y)\subset \operatorname{Supp}(D).

Proof

(1). It suffices to prove the “only if” part.

Step 1. Projective reduction

We may assume that ff is projective and X,YX,Y are quasi-projective.

Proof

By Chow’s lemma, we obtain a projective birational morphisms π:X′→X\pi:X'\to X and f′:X′→Yf':X'\to Y. Let BB be an effective divisor on X′X', then π*B\pi_*B is again effective. So we may assume that ff is projective. Let U⊂YU\subset Y be an affine open subset which meets each component of f*Df_*D. Replacing YY by UU we may assume that YY is affine. Then X,YX,Y are quasi-projective.

□

Step 2. An antiample divisor

Then there exists an effective divisor EE on XX which is ff-antiample and EE contains each exceptional divisor.

Proof

Let ℒ\mathcal{L} be an ff-ample line bundle on XX. Since YY is affine by assumption, there exists a nonzero element ss in H0(Y,f*ℒ−1)=H0(X,ℒ−1)H^0(Y,f_*\mathcal{L}^{-1})=H^0(X,\mathcal{L}^{-1}). Let E:=div⁡(s)E:=\operatorname{div}(s), we see that −E-E is ff-ample.

To show that EE contains each exceptional divisor, note that each exceptional divisor is covered by curves contracted by ff. Let CC be such a curve. If C⊄Supp⁡(E)C\not\subset \operatorname{Supp}(E), the intersection number E⋅CE\cdot C must be non-negative. But −E-E is relatively ample over YY and hence we have E⋅C<0E\cdot C<0, a contradiction. So we have C⊂Supp⁡(E)C\subset \operatorname{Supp}(E) and therefore EE contains each exceptional divisor.

□

Step 3. The intersection contradiction

The negative part of DD is exceptional. Add the smallest multiple of EE needed for effectivity, then compare intersections on a curve contracted by ff.

Proof

Let D=D+−D−D=D^+-D^- where D+,D−D^+,D^- are effective. We see that D−D^- is exceptional since f*Df_*D is effective. Let ee be the minimal non-negative number such that D+eED+eE is effective.

Then there exists an prime divisor FF whose coefficient is positive in DD but is zero in D+eED+eE. Then there exists a curve C⊂FC\subset F which is contracted by ff and not contained in the support of D+eED+eE. Hecne we have (D+eE)⋅C≥0(D+eE)\cdot C\ge 0. But −D-D is ff-nef and −E-E is ff-ample, we obtain that (D+eE)⋅C<0(D+eE)\cdot C<0 a contradiction.

□

(2). If there exists a fiber f−1(y)f^{-1}(y) such that f−1(y)∩Supp⁡(D)≠∅f^{-1}(y)\cap \operatorname{Supp}(D)\not=\emptyset and f−1(y)⊄Supp⁡(D)f^{-1}(y)\not\subset \operatorname{Supp}(D), then we can find a curve CC in the fiber that intersects the support of DD but is not contained in it. Then we have D⋅C>0D\cdot C>0 Contradiction.

□
