Negativity Lemma

Theorem. Let f:X→Yf:X\to Y be a proper birational morphism between normal varieties. Let DD be a ℝ\mathbb R-Cartier divisor on XX such that −D-D is nef over YY. Then:

  1. DD is effective if and only if f*Df_*D is effective.

  2. Assume that DD is effective. Then for every y∈Yy\in Y, either f−1(y)∩Supp⁡(D)=∅f^{-1}(y)\cap \operatorname{Supp}(D)=\emptyset or f−1(y)⊂Supp⁡(D)f^{-1}(y)\subset \operatorname{Supp}(D).

Proof of (1)

It suffices to prove the “only if” part.

Step 1.We may assume that ff is projective and X,YX,Y are quasi-projective.

Step 2.Then there exists an effective divisor EE which is ff-antiample and EE contains each exceptional divisor.

Step 3. Note that the negative part of DD is exceptional. Let ee be the minimal non-negative number such that D+eED+eE is effective then there exists a curve CC contracted by ff such that (D+eE)⋅C≥0(D+eE)\cdot C\ge 0. But note that −D-D is ff-nef and −E-E is ff-ample and hence we get a contradiction.

Proof of (2)

If there exists a fiber f−1(y)f^{-1}(y) such that f−1(y)∩Supp⁡(D)≠∅f^{-1}(y)\cap \operatorname{Supp}(D)\not=\emptyset and f−1(y)⊄Supp⁡(D)f^{-1}(y)\not\subset \operatorname{Supp}(D), then we can find a curve CC in the fiber that intersects the support of DD but is not contained in it. Then we have D⋅C>0D\cdot C>0 Contradiction.

Details of proof of (1)

Step 1.

By Chow’s lemma, we obtain a projective birational morphisms π:X′→X\pi:X'\to X and f′:X′→Yf':X'\to Y. Let BB be an effective divisor on X′X', then π*B\pi_*B is again effective. So we may assume that ff is projective. Let U⊂YU\subset Y be an affine open subset which meets each component of f*Df_*D. Replacing YY by UU we may assume that YY is affine. Then X,YX,Y are quasi-projective.

↑ Back to the proof of (1) \quad ↓ Next step

Step 2.

Let HH be an ample divisor on YY which contains each center of exceptional divisors. Then we have f*H=H̃+Ef^*H=\widetilde{H}+E Where H̃\widetilde{H} is the birational transform of HH and EE contains each exceptional divisor. We see that E∼f−H̃E\sim_{f} -\widetilde{H} and hence −E-E is ff-ample.

↑ Back to the proof of (1) \quad ↓ Next step

Step 3.

Let D=D+−D−D=D^+-D^- where D+,D−D^+,D^- are effective. We see that D−D^- is exceptional since f*Df_*D is effective. Let ee be the minimal non-negative number such that D+eED+eE is effective. Then there exists an prime divisor FF whose coefficient is positive in DD but is zero in D+eED+eE. Then there exists a curve C⊂FC\subset F which is contracted by ff and not contained in the support of D+eED+eE. Hecne we have (D+eE)⋅C≥0(D+eE)\cdot C\ge 0. But −D-D is ff-nef and −E-E is ff-ample, we obtain that (D+eE)⋅C<0(D+eE)\cdot C<0 a contradiction. ↑ Back to the proof of (1)
