Cartier divisor

Let XX be a variety over field kk. Here, we develop the correspondence between Cartier divisors, the line bundle associated with a Cartier divisor, and the embedding of such bundles into the function field. We begin by considering codimension-one closed subschemes of XX that are locally defined by a single function.

Consider an open cover X=⋃iUiX=\bigcup_i U_i, and pick fi∈Γ(Ui,𝒪X)⊂K(X)f_i\in \Gamma(U_i,\mathcal{O}_X)\subset K(X) satisfying fifj∈Γ(Ui∩Uj,𝒪X)×\frac{f_i}{f_j}\in \Gamma(U_i\cap U_j,\mathcal{O}_X)^{\times}. According to our choice, the zero set of fif_i V(fi)={x∈Ui|(fi)x∈𝔪x⊂𝒪X,x}⊂Ui\begin{align*} V(f_i)=\{x\in U_i|(f_{i})_x\in \mathfrak{m}_x\subset \mathcal{O}_{X,x}\}\subset U_i \end{align*} is a closed subscheme in UiU_i satisfy V(fi)∩Uj=V(fj)∩UiV(f_i)\cap U_j=V(f_j)\cap U_i. So we obtain a closed subscheme DD on XX. We denote D={(Ui,fi)}iD=\{(U_i,f_i)\}_i.

This collection of data induces a line bundle on XX. For each UiU_i, we can define a subsheaf of the function field of UiU_i, that is, 𝒪Ui⋅1fi⊂𝒦X|Ui\mathcal{O}_{U_i}\cdot \frac{1}{f_i} \subset\mathcal{K}_X|_{U_i}, with transition functions φij:(𝒪Ui⋅1fi)|Ui∩Uj≅(𝒪Uj⋅1fj)|Ui∩Ujsifi↦sifi.\begin{align*} \varphi_{ij}:\big(\mathcal{O}_{U_i}\cdot \frac{1}{f_i}\big)|_{U_i\cap U_j}&\cong \big(\mathcal{O}_{U_j}\cdot \frac{1}{f_j}\big)|_{U_i\cap U_j} \\ \frac{s_i}{f_i}&\mapsto \frac{s_i}{f_i}. \end{align*} Therefore these locally defined sheaves can be glued together to get a line bundle 𝒪X(D)\mathcal{O}_X(D) on XX with isomorphism ⁡(D)|Ui≅𝒪Ui⋅1fi\mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{O}_X}}(D)|_{U_i}\cong \mathcal{O}_{U_i}\cdot \frac{1}{f_i}. Each section s∈Γ(V,𝒪X(D))s\in\Gamma(V,\mathcal{O}_X(D)) is of the form s={(Ui∩V,gifi)}s=\{(U_i\cap V,\frac{g_i}{f_i})\} such that gifi=gjfj\frac{g_i}{f_i}=\frac{g_j}{f_j} on Ui∩Uj∩VU_i\cap U_j\cap V. Similarly, by gluing the local sheaf 𝒪Ui⋅fi\mathcal{O}_{U_i}\cdot f_i together, we obtain an idea sheaf ℐD⊂𝒪X\mathcal{I}_D\subset \mathcal{O}_X. We can see that ℐD\mathcal{I}_D is also a line bundle such that 𝒪X(D)⊗ℐD≅\mathcal{O}_X(D)\otimes \mathcal{I}_D\cong \mathop{\mathrm{\mathcal{O}_X}} and D=V(ℐD)D=V(\mathcal{I}_D).

Let’s descrive the embedding of 𝒪X(D)\mathcal{O}_X(D) into the sheaf of rational functions 𝒦X\mathcal{K}_X. Note that we have an injection 𝒪X↪𝒦X.\begin{align*} \mathcal{O}_X\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}_X. \end{align*} Tensoring 𝒪X(D)\mathcal{O}_X(D), we have 𝒪X(D)↪𝒪X(D)⊗𝒦X.\begin{align*} \mathcal{O}_X(D)\hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(D)\otimes \mathcal{K}_X. \end{align*} If D={(Ui,fi)}D=\{(U_i,f_i)\}, then we obtain an isomorphism as following 𝒪X(D)⊗𝒦X≅𝒦X{(Ui∩V,gifi⊗a)}↦{(Ui∩V,a⋅gifi)}.\begin{align*} \mathcal{O}_X(D)\otimes \mathcal{K}_X&\cong \mathcal{K}_X\\ \{(U_i\cap V,\frac{g_i}{f_i}\otimes a)\}&\mapsto \{(U_i\cap V,a\cdot \frac{g_i}{f_i})\}. \end{align*} Note that these locally defined functions {(Ui∩V,a⋅gifi)}\{(U_i\cap V,a\cdot \frac{g_i}{f_i})\} patch together to define a rational function a⋅gf∈Γ(V,𝒦X)=K(X)a\cdot\frac{g}{f}\in \Gamma(V,\mathcal{K}_X)=K(X) such that (a⋅gf)|Ui=a⋅gifi(a\cdot\frac{g}{f})|_{U_i}=a\cdot\frac{g_i}{f_i}. Weil divisor

Let XX be a variety over field kk. Recall that an effective Cartier divisor of XX can be viewed as a closed subscheme of XX such that it is locally cut out by a single function. But not every codimensional-one closed integral subscheme is of this form. In fact, we have

Proposition 2.1. Let XX be the variety as above. Then the following are equivalent.

(1) XX is locally factorial.

(2) Every closed integral subscheme of XX is locally defined by a single function.

This proposition is just the geometric version of the following lemma

Lemma 2.2. AA a noetherian integral domain. Then AA is factorial if and only if every prime of height 11 is a principal ideal.

Now, let us define the associated sheaf of a Weil divisor.

Definition 2.3. Let XX be a normal variety, K(X)K(X) the function field. Given a Weil divisor DD, define 𝒪X(D)\mathcal{O}_X(D) to be the sheaf such that Γ(V,𝒪X(D))={f∈K(X)|div⁡(f)|V+D|V≥0}∪{0}.\begin{align*} \Gamma(V,\mathcal{O}_X(D))=\{f\in K(X)|\operatorname{div}(f)|_V+D|_V\ge 0\}\cup \{0\}. \end{align*} By $"\ge"$, I mean that div⁡(f)|V+D|V\operatorname{div}(f)|_V+D|_V is effective on VV.

Note that 𝒪X(D)\mathcal{O}_X(D) is in fact a coherent sheaf, though I omit the proof here.
