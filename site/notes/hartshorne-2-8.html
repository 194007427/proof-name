Differentials

Let RR be a ring and AA be a RR-algebra. An RR-derivation of AA into an AA-module MM is map d:A→Md:A\to M such that (1)D(sa+tb)=sD(a)+tD(b),a,b∈A and s,t∈R;(2)D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a),a,b∈A(Leibniz rule).\begin{align*} &(1) \ D(sa+tb)=sD(a)+tD(b), \quad a,b\in A \text{ and } s,t\in R;\\ &(2) \ D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a), \quad a,b\in A \ (\text{Leibniz rule}). \end{align*} The set of all RR-derivations from AA into MM is denoted by Der⁡R(A,M)\operatorname{Der}_R(A,M).

The module of Kahler differentials defined as follows: ΩA/R:=(⨁a∈AA⋅da)/N,\Omega_{A/R}:=\big(\bigoplus_{a\in A}A\cdot da\big)/N, where NN is an AA-submodule generated by the following elements: (1)d(sa+tb)−sda−tdb,a,b∈A and s,t∈R;(2)d(ab)−adb−bda,a,b∈A.\begin{align*} &(1) \ d(sa+tb)-sda-tdb, \quad a,b\in A \text{ and } s,t\in R;\\ &(2) \ d(ab)-adb-bda, \quad a,b\in A. \end{align*} Note that d(r)=0d(r)=0 for all r∈Rr\in R. The derivation is defined by a↦daa\mapsto da. This module satisfies the following universal property: for any AA-module MM, and for any RR-derivation D:A→MD:A\to M, there exists a unique AA-linear map f:ΩA/R→Mf:\Omega_{A/R}\to M such that D=f∘dD=f\circ d. In short, we have Der⁡R(A,M)≅Hom⁡A(ΩA/R,M).\begin{align*} \operatorname{Der}_R(A,M)\cong \operatorname{Hom}_A(\Omega_{A/R},M). \end{align*}

Local coordinate

Proposition 12. A local Noetherian ring (R,𝔪)(R,\mathfrak{m}) with residue field kk is called regular if the following equivalent conditions are satisfied

(1) dim⁡R=dim⁡k𝔪/𝔪2\operatorname{dim}R=\operatorname{dim}_k\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2;

(2) 𝔪\mathfrak{m} can be generated by dim⁡R\operatorname{dim}R elements.

Proof. (1)⟹\implies(2): By Nakayama’s lemma.

(2)⟹\implies(1): The condition implies that dim⁡k𝔪/𝔪2≤dim⁡R\operatorname{dim}_k\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2\le \operatorname{dim}R. ◻

Remark 13. Let kk be perfect and (R,𝔪,k)(R,\mathfrak{m},k) be a Noetherian local integral domain over kk with quotient field KK. Assume furthermore that RR is a localization of a finitely generated kk-algebra. Then the conditions in Proposition 12 are equivalent to the following equation dim⁡R=dim⁡k(ΩR/k⊗Rk)=dim⁡KΩK/k.\operatorname{dim}R=\operatorname{dim}_k\big(\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_Rk\big)=\operatorname{dim}_K\Omega_{K/k}.

Definition 14 (local coordinate). Let (R,𝔪)(R,\mathfrak{m}) be a Noetherian local ring of dimension dd.

(1) A system of parameters of RR is a sequence of elements x1,⋯,xd∈𝔪x_1,\cdots ,x_d\in \mathfrak{m} which generates an ideal of definition of RR, that is, (x1,⋯,xd)=𝔪\sqrt{(x_1,\cdots,x_d)}=\mathfrak{m};

(2) if there exist x1,⋯,xd∈𝔪x_1,\cdots,x_d\in \mathfrak{m} such that 𝔪=(x1,⋯,xd)\mathfrak{m}=(x_1,\cdots,x_d), then RR is a regular local ring by Proposition 12 and we call x1,⋯,xdx_1,\cdots,x_d a local coordinate (or regular system of parameters, cf. [Stacks, Tag 00KU])

(1)⟹\implies(3): Note that dim⁡R/(x1,⋯,xi)=n−i\operatorname{dim}R/(x_1,\cdots,x_i)=n-i and the images in R/(x1,⋯,xi)R/(x_1,\cdots,x_i) of x1,⋯,xnx_1,\cdots,x_n generate the maximal ideal of R/(x1,⋯,xi)R/(x_1,\cdots,x_i). Then use Proposition 12.

Theorem 23 (Bertini theorem). Let XX be a nonsingular closed subvariety of ℙkn\mathbb{P}_k^n, where kk is an algebraically closed field. Then there exists a hyperplane H⊆ℙknH \subseteq \mathbb{P}_k^n, not containing XX, and such that the scheme H∩XH \cap X is regular at every point. Furthermore, the set of hyperplanes with this property forms an open dense subset of the complete linear system ℙ(Γ(ℙn,H))\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^n,H)).

Proof. If XX is contained in every hyperplane, then XX must be empty. Thus we may assume that there exists a hyperplane H⊂ℙnH\subset \mathbb{P}^n such that X⊄HX\not\subset H. If H={(Ui,si)}H=\{(U_i,s_i)\}, let DD be the pullback of HH, we have D={(Ui∩X,si)}D=\{(U_i\cap X,s_i)\} (omitting the restriction of rational functions). Pick x∈Xx\in X and UiU_i such that x∈Uix\in U_i, we then obtain the following maps

Setting φx:Γ(ℙn,H)→𝒪X,x/𝔪x2\varphi_x:\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^n,H)\to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}^2_x. First, observe that 𝒪X,x/𝔪x2=𝒪X,x/𝔪x⊕𝔪x/𝔪x2\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}^2_x=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_x\oplus \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}^2_x, so it suffices to show that φx\varphi_x maps onto kk and 𝔪x/𝔪x2\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}^2_x. Note that φx(1/si)=1∈k\varphi_x(1/s_i)=1\in k so the surjectivity onto kk is immediate. Then, observe that the map 𝒪ℙn,x→𝒪X,x\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n,x}\to \mathcal{O}_{X,x} is surjective and hence induces a surjection 𝔪ℙn,x→𝔪x/𝔪x2\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{P}^n,x}\to \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}^2_x, which shows the surjectivity onto 𝔪x/𝔪x2\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}^2_x.
